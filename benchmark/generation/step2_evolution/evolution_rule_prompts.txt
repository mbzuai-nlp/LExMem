RULE-MATCHED GRAPH CONTINUATION PROMPTS

USAGE AND CONTROLLER
Send the SHARED PROMPT plus one selected template. Fill all placeholders.
Match conditions and checks below are controller instructions, not model prompts.

Scan nodes in order. An edge becomes available when both endpoints have appeared.
Consider a matched edge only at its first available order; do not resample old
edges at every later node. Check conditions against the prefix, not the future.
Apply a configurable trigger probability to each eligible match; if triggered,
select one eligible template and make at most one continuation call at this
position. Keep the prefix through the cutoff and let the
model regenerate the complete future. After validation, resume at cutoff + 1.
Stop when scanning reaches the current graph's end without another trigger.
No maximum rounds or separate stopping probability are specified.

SHARED PROMPT
=============
Continue this content-free graph with the selected development and return the
complete graph, including its unchanged history.

Original seeds (one goal and 3-5 proposals):
{{SEED_INPUT_JSON}}

Immutable prefix through order {{CUTOFF_ORDER}}:
{{PREFIX_JSON}}

Requirements:
- Preserve every prefix node and edge. Add no edges between two prefix nodes.
  Regenerate the future; the discarded suffix need not be reconstructed.
- Include exactly the supplied goal and proposals, including those not yet in
  the prefix. Add no descriptions, domain text, status/history fields, dialogue,
  QA, or extra metadata.
- Encourage multiple branches, depth, and broad exploration without padding.
  Place the requested development naturally, possibly after other exploration,
  then continue its consequences and complete the remaining graph.
- Templates specify outcomes, not fixed node chains. Choose the intervening
  structure, node count, and connections freely within the schema.
- New nodes have unique neutral IDs and consecutive orders after the cutoff.
  Order is dialogue chronology. Activities precede their new outputs; evaluated
  targets precede their new evaluating activities. Revisions/revivals follow
  their targets.
- Keep a single exploration tree rooted at g1 and left-to-right DFS preorder.
  The root has a null parent; every other node has one earlier existing parent.
  Proposals are direct children of g1 in seed order, each linked from g1 by
  decompose. Continue from the traversal frontier without reopening closed
  subtrees; use semantic edges to refer to earlier topics across branches.
  Exploration parents specify traversal only, not semantic relationships.
- Preserve old findings. New evidence does not automatically invalidate related
  nodes or settle conflicting evidence. Account for explicit prerequisites
  without silently rewriting historical dependencies.
- revise does not imply adoption or abandonment. refute alone does not justify
  revive, which requires a previously rejected, deferred, or inactive target.
  Evidence supporting a decision does not specify what that decision selected.

Node types:
goal, proposal, question, hypothesis, plan, activity, evidence, decision, artifact.

Allowed relations (source -> target):
- address: proposal/hypothesis/plan/activity -> question/goal; addresses the target.
- produce: activity -> evidence/artifact; generates an output.
- depend_on: dependent -> actual prerequisite, not generic association.
- evaluate: activity -> hypothesis/proposal/artifact; assesses the target.
- support: evidence -> hypothesis/proposal/decision; provides grounds for it.
- refute: evidence -> hypothesis/proposal; contradicts the target.
- decompose: goal -> proposal; proposal -> proposal; plan -> activity.
- revise: revised version -> earlier version; substantively modifies it.
- revive: evidence/decision -> proposal/hypothesis; reopens consideration.

Return only JSON with nodes and edges. Each node has exactly id, type, order,
exploration_parent_id; each edge has exactly source, relation, target.
Check prefix preservation, seed coverage, valid references and relation directions,
DFS/chronological order, and completion of the selected task. No self-edges,
duplicate edges, circular prerequisites, Markdown fences, or explanations.

RULE S: SUPPORTED TARGET
=======================
Match: {{SUPPORT_EVIDENCE_ID}} support {{TARGET_ID}}; evidence -> hypothesis or
proposal; no refute edge to the target in the prefix. Exclude decision targets.

S1 — Later counterevidence
Prompt:
{{SUPPORT_EVIDENCE_ID}} supports {{TARGET_ID}}. In the continuation, refute
{{TARGET_ID}} while preserving its earlier support, and complete the graph.
Check: a new evidence node refutes the target.

RULE F: REFUTED TARGET
=====================
Match: {{REFUTING_EVIDENCE_ID}} refute {{TARGET_ID}}; evidence -> hypothesis or
proposal; no support edge to the target in the prefix.

F1 — Later support
Prompt:
{{REFUTING_EVIDENCE_ID}} refutes {{TARGET_ID}}. In the continuation, provide
support for {{TARGET_ID}} while preserving the earlier refutation, and complete
the graph. This need not resolve the conflicting findings.
Check: a new evidence node supports the target.

RULE RH: REVISED HYPOTHESIS
==========================
Match: {{REVISED_HYPOTHESIS_ID}} revise {{EARLIER_HYPOTHESIS_ID}}; both are
hypotheses; revised node is later.

RH1 — Further revision
Prompt:
{{REVISED_HYPOTHESIS_ID}} revises {{EARLIER_HYPOTHESIS_ID}}. Let subsequent
developments lead to a further revision of {{REVISED_HYPOTHESIS_ID}}, and complete
the graph while preserving the earlier versions and their relationships.
Check: a new hypothesis revises the matched revised hypothesis.

RH2 — Later findings favor the earlier version
Eligibility: no refute edge to the revised hypothesis is present in the prefix.
Prompt:
{{REVISED_HYPOTHESIS_ID}} revises {{EARLIER_HYPOTHESIS_ID}}. Let later findings
support {{EARLIER_HYPOTHESIS_ID}} and refute {{REVISED_HYPOTHESIS_ID}}, and complete
the graph. This does not imply automatic rollback, revival, or adoption.
Check: new evidence supports the earlier hypothesis and new evidence refutes the
revised hypothesis; these may be the same or different evidence nodes.

RULE RA: REVISED ARTIFACT
========================
Match: {{REVISED_ARTIFACT_ID}} revise {{EARLIER_ARTIFACT_ID}}; both are artifacts;
the revised node is later.

RA1 — Another artifact version
Prompt:
{{REVISED_ARTIFACT_ID}} revises {{EARLIER_ARTIFACT_ID}}. Let subsequent
developments lead to a further revision of {{REVISED_ARTIFACT_ID}}, and complete
the graph. Preserve earlier versions; revision does not imply deployment.
Check: a new artifact revises the matched revised artifact.

RULE V: REOPENED TARGET
======================
Match: {{REVIVAL_SOURCE_ID}} revive {{TARGET_ID}}; evidence/decision ->
hypothesis/proposal. No evidence appearing after the revival source already
supports or refutes the target in the prefix.
Use an existing validated revival annotation. refute/revise alone cannot establish
prior inactivity; these templates do not fill that gap in the source graph.

V1 — Support after reopening
Prompt:
{{REVIVAL_SOURCE_ID}} reopens {{TARGET_ID}}. Let the continuation provide support
for {{TARGET_ID}}, and complete the graph. Preserve earlier findings without
assuming all previous objections have been resolved.
Check: a new evidence node supports the reopened target.

V2 — Refutation after reopening
Prompt:
{{REVIVAL_SOURCE_ID}} reopens {{TARGET_ID}}. Let the continuation refute
{{TARGET_ID}}, and complete the graph. Preserve the reopening as part of history.
Check: a new evidence node refutes the reopened target.

ACCEPTANCE
==========
The requested outcome must occur after the cutoff; old findings cannot satisfy
it. Check shared structural constraints and the selected outcome, then review
whether the surrounding continuation makes it plausible. Do not require a fixed
activity/evidence/decision chain or a fixed number of intermediate nodes.
Outcome presence alone does not guarantee coherence or valid future domain text.
Accept only a complete graph; failed candidates do not replace the current graph.
